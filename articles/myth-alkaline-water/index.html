Myth: Alkaline Water Is Healthier

Water sold at a pH of eight or nine is said to neutralise acid in the body, slow ageing and keep disease away, and a machine that makes it at home can cost several thousand dollars. What alkaline water is, what happens to it in the first ten seconds after it is swallowed, why the body holds its blood at the same pH whatever is drunk, what the reviews of the evidence found, where the idea came from and what became of the man who made it famous, and the awkward fact that the water in most kitchen taps is already alkaline.

12 November 2026 · By Cesar Chu Ortega, founder of Aguato · 8 min read

Share

The machine sits on the counter beside the kitchen tap, about the size of a toaster stood on end, with a hose from the tap going in and a steel spout coming out and a panel of buttons that light up in different colours. Press one and the water runs out at a pH of eight and a half, for drinking. Press another and it comes out at nine and a half, for cooking. A third gives acid water, for washing the face. The person who owns it paid somewhere between one and five thousand dollars, usually to a friend or a relative who sells them, and was told at the demonstration that the body of anyone who eats a modern diet is too acid, that acid is behind tiredness, ageing, weight and worse, and that drinking alkaline water every day will bring the body back into balance. Supermarkets sell the same promise by the bottle, at a pH printed large on the label.

The claim is that alkaline water is healthier than ordinary water. This article is about what alkaline water is, what the body does with it, what the research found, where the idea came from, and what is already coming out of the tap.

What it is

The pH scale, which the pH article on this site explains, runs from 0 to 14. Seven is neutral, lower is acid, higher is alkaline, and each step is a factor of ten. Alkaline water for drinking is sold at about 8 to 9.5.

It is made in one of two ways. The bottled kind usually has minerals added, a little bicarbonate, calcium or magnesium or potassium, until the pH rises. The machine, called an ioniser, works by electricity. Tap water flows past two metal plates with a current between them, and the dissolved minerals that every tap water contains are pulled toward one plate or the other. The water drawn off near one plate ends up with more of the alkaline minerals in it and a higher pH; the water near the other plate ends up acid and goes down the drain. Pure water cannot be ionised this way at all, because the effect depends on what is dissolved in it.

Either way the result is water with a high pH and a very small amount of dissolved alkali behind it. That second part matters more than the label admits. The pH says how alkaline a liquid is at this moment. It does not say how much acid the liquid can soak up before its pH falls, which chemists call its buffering capacity, and a glass of ionised water at pH 9 has almost none. A teaspoon of baking soda stirred into a glass of water has thousands of times more.

The first ten seconds

The first thing a mouthful of alkaline water meets is the stomach. The stomach makes hydrochloric acid, about a litre and a half of gastric juice a day, and holds its contents at a pH of about 1.5 to 3.5. That is the point of it: the acid kills most of what is swallowed with food and starts the breakdown of protein.

On the scale, the distance between pH 9 and pH 2 is seven steps, a factor of ten million, and the stomach's acid is concentrated where the water's alkali is thin. The glass of alkaline water is neutralised and then made acid within seconds of arriving. If someone did manage to swallow enough alkali to raise the pH of the stomach for a while, by taking an antacid tablet, which contains far more than any water, the stomach responds by making more acid.

What leaves the stomach is then made alkaline again by the body itself. The pancreas pours bicarbonate into the top of the small intestine to neutralise the stomach's acid, every day, whether the person drank alkaline water, tap water or lemon juice.

The blood

Behind the stomach is a second line that the claim would have to cross, and it is one of the most tightly guarded numbers in the body. Blood is held at a pH between 7.35 and 7.45. It is slightly alkaline already, which is a fact the sellers tend to leave out.

Three systems hold it there. Dissolved bicarbonate in the blood soaks up acid or alkali the moment it appears. The lungs adjust within minutes, because carbon dioxide dissolved in blood is an acid and breathing faster removes it. The kidneys finish the work over hours, by passing any surplus acid or alkali into the urine. The body produces a large amount of acid every day simply by living, mostly as carbon dioxide, and disposes of all of it without help.

This is why the test strips sold with the machines appear to prove that they work. A person who takes in extra alkali will pass more alkaline urine for a few hours. The strip shows the kidneys doing their job, getting rid of the surplus so that the blood stays where it was. It does not show a body that has become more alkaline.

When blood pH does move, it is because something is seriously wrong: the lungs or the kidneys are failing, or diabetes is out of control. A shift of two or three tenths of a unit is a medical emergency. It is not something a drink causes or cures.

What the studies found

The strongest version of the claim is about cancer, and it has been checked. In 2016 two Canadian researchers searched the medical literature for every study of alkaline water or an alkaline diet and cancer. They screened more than eight thousand papers and found one that met the basic standard for a clinical study, and that one found no link. Their conclusion was that promoting alkaline water or diet to the public for the prevention or treatment of cancer is not justified.

Two smaller claims have something behind them, and it is worth being exact about how much. A laboratory experiment in 2012 showed that water at pH 8.8 switches off pepsin, a stomach enzyme that does damage when it is carried up into the throat in people with acid reflux. That was done in a dish. It suggests that alkaline water might soothe reflux for the moment it is in the throat, as an antacid does, and no proper trial in patients has tested it. And a few small studies, several of them paid for by companies that sell the water, have reported slightly better hydration after exercise. The differences were small, and plain water also hydrates.

Mineral waters that are naturally rich in bicarbonate and calcium have been shown to do modest good for bones in some studies. That is the effect of the minerals, which are nutrients, and it has nothing to do with a machine on the counter.

Where it came from

The idea that food and drink make the body acid or alkaline is about a century old, and it rests on a real observation: when food is burned to ash in a laboratory, the ash of meat and grain is acid and the ash of fruit and vegetables is alkaline. From there it was a short step to the claim that an acid diet makes an acid body. The step is wrong, for the reason given above, though the diet that the theory recommends, more vegetables and less meat, is a good one for other reasons.

The modern version was made famous by an American author whose books on the subject, beginning in 2002, sold millions of copies in many languages. He ran a clinic on a ranch in California where people with cancer paid for alkaline treatments, some of them given into a vein. In 2016 he was convicted of practising medicine without a licence, and served a jail sentence. In 2018 a jury ordered him to pay more than a hundred million dollars to a woman with breast cancer who had given up conventional treatment on his advice.

The machines have their own history. They came from Japan, where they were approved as medical devices in the 1960s for minor stomach complaints, and they are sold in the rest of the world largely through networks in which each buyer is encouraged to recruit the next.

What is in the tap

The last fact is the one that should settle whether to spend the money. Most tap water is already alkaline. Water companies keep the pH of what they supply at about 7.5 to 8.5 on purpose, and often add lime or a little caustic soda to get it there, because slightly alkaline water does not dissolve metal pipes. What happens when that is neglected is in the lead article on this site. Water drawn through chalk or limestone, such as London's, arrives alkaline and well buffered without anyone doing anything. The World Health Organization sets no health limit for pH in drinking water at all, high or low, because within the range that is supplied it has no direct effect on the person drinking it.

For a healthy person alkaline water is also harmless, apart from the price. People with kidney disease are sometimes advised to avoid water with a lot of added minerals. One brand sold in the United States was recalled in 2021 after it was linked to cases of liver failure, one of them fatal, and the cause was a contaminant in the product, which had nothing to do with its pH.

Verdict

False. Alkaline water is neutralised by the stomach within seconds, and the body holds the pH of its blood steady by its own means, whatever is drunk. The reviews of the evidence have found no benefit for cancer or for any serious disease. A possible soothing effect on reflux has been shown only in a laboratory dish.

It is water, and water is good for you. If the taste is pleasant, which some people find it is, that is a fair reason to buy a bottle, and no reason to buy a machine.

A pH of 7.4 in the blood, about 2 in the stomach, and a kitchen tap that was slightly alkaline all along.
